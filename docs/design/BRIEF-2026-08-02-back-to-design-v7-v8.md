# Back to design — six questions from building v7/v8

**2026-08-02. Response to the v7 and v8 packs.**

The packs are good and mostly land. v7's headline overturned the premise we gave
you — *"it was never the item count, it was twenty-six cards of chrome around
eleven items"* — and that is a better diagnosis than the one in our brief. The
three tiers retire our "always render" rule cleanly while keeping both honesty
invariants intact. We are adopting them.

Two things you flagged were verified in the codebase within minutes of reading:
`#5B5B68` was live as a text token on two dark surfaces at ~2.5:1 (now fixed),
and the web verb-key suspension you specified was already built that way.

What follows is only what we cannot resolve without you. Everything else is
being implemented as drawn.

---

## Q1 — Define "sender identity" precisely (blocking, and it has a safety cost)

v7: *"Stacks group by sender identity, not address — HSBC's three addresses are
one stack."*

We built grouping the week before and deliberately refused this. Our rule is
exact address, plus thread id. We explicitly rejected same-domain grouping with
this reasoning: on the owner's mailbox `@notification.hsbc.com.hk` is three
robots, but on the next customer's mailbox the same rule folds a colleague's
personal note into their employer's billing robot. A wrongly merged pair of real
obligations is a serious failure, and merges are the one operation where a false
positive hides something rather than merely showing it.

Both positions are defensible. They cannot both hold. **We need the rule stated
precisely enough to implement**, and specifically:

- Is identity the **display name** (`HSBC` across three addresses)? That breaks
  on `HSBC` vs `HSBC eStatement and eAdvice`, which are different display names
  on the owner's real data.
- Is it the **registrable domain**? That is the one we rejected.
- Is it **domain AND structurally-automated sender** (a `no-reply@`-shaped local
  part)? This is the version we think is safe, because it can never group a
  human, and we would like to propose it — but it means a stack only ever
  contains robots, which may not be what the frame intends.

Whatever the rule, our constraint stands: **a stack must never be able to
contain a human's message.**

## Q2 — What is HQ's order *before* the landing screen ships?

v7 puts needs-you first on HQ, "deliberately reversing canonical order, because
you arrived via a sentence that already delivered the receipts."

That justification is sound and entirely **conditional on the landing surface
existing**. These are separate builds and the landing screen is the larger one.
If we ship the reorder first, HQ opens on the owner's obligations with no
receipts anywhere — which is precisely the inbox feeling the delivered-first
rule was introduced to remove.

**Question:** do you want (a) the reorder held until the landing screen ships
together with it, or (b) an interim HQ order for the gap? We lean to (a) and
will hold unless told otherwise.

## Q3 — What does a row show when comprehension failed?

v7 §B specifies three slots: verb phrase, mission chip, the one thing that
decides *when*. It assumes a verb phrase exists.

Ours are generated by a model reading the arrival, and that model is allowed to
fail or to return low confidence. When it does, we deliberately keep the
original subject line rather than emit a worse title — the alternative is
inventing an action the message does not contain, which we will not do.

So some rows will read `Email from CIPA: 2026 Annual Return Due…` — exactly the
shape v7 says a row must never be. **What should that row look like?** Options
we see: show it as-is and mark it unread-by-Cue; show the subject with a
"couldn't summarise" affordance; or route it somewhere else entirely. We would
rather you decide than pick one and have it read as a bug.

Related: what does a **stack row** show when the stack contains such an item?
"1 may matter" is defined for actionable content; this is a different state —
Cue does not know what it is.

## Q4 — "Read-only to start" may not be ours to offer

P4's strongest move is removing the real objection: *"the objection is never
effort — it's handing your inbox to something that might send from it."* We
agree, and we want it.

**But we may not be able to honour it.** Connectors route through Composio, and
the OAuth scope set is defined in Composio's auth config per toolkit, not by us.
We control scopes on our own direct-OAuth path, but that is not the path
connectors use. We are verifying with Composio now.

**If read-only Gmail is not available, that frame promises something the product
cannot do** — which is worse than the wall it replaces, because the promise is
specifically about trust. We will confirm within a day. If it is unavailable, we
will need a fallback frame that is honest about full access while still not
reading as a demand.

## Q5 — Missions do not currently start anything

v7 and the canonical deck both treat missions as live: rings, cycles, agents at
work. On the owner's instance a created mission **sits there** — nothing
recruits or starts an agent. This is under investigation on our side and is not
a design defect.

Flagging it because several frames imply motion that does not yet exist, and
because the fix may change what mission surfaces need to show (for example, if
missions need an explicit start, that is a control no frame currently has).

## Q6 — Confirm you know mobile already shipped against the older spec

v8 supersedes canonical K2 and the tab bar in `05-mobile-native`. Both are
**built and shipped** — the mobile app follows the v3 NATIVE spec today.

So v8 is a retrofit over working code, not a greenfield. That is fine and we
think the direction is right — *"if a future surface disagrees with mobile about
what deserves weight, mobile is probably right"* is a good rule. We just want to
be sure the supersession is intentional with that context, particularly dropping
the `+` from the tab bar, which users of the current build have muscle memory
for.

## Not questions — things we are simply doing

- Adopting the three tiers, including demoting our honest empty states from
  cards to Tier 3 lines. Both invariants survive: never silently absent, never a
  fake number.
- Moving provenance off the row and behind it. Your reasoning is better than
  ours: provenance answers a question most rows are never asked, and putting it
  on the line is part of what made a hundred rows unreadable.
- The landing surface, with the delivery sentence as the only door and prompts
  drawn from real state.
- `/` and `⌘K` for search, `⌘F` never intercepted, real URLs per surface.
- Life never shareable; leash proposals never travel.
